# AdSense quality and monetization plan

No AdSense application or ad installation has been performed. There is no approval guarantee, minimum-page-count claim, revenue forecast or claim that a particular family is automatically eligible.

## Official guidance -> project decision

| Official source | Relevant principle | UtilityPilot implementation decision |
|---|---|---|
| [Site readiness](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563?hl=en) | Original, relevant content and usable navigation | Complete real tools and explanatory pages before submission |
| [Program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en) | Follow publisher rules, including legitimate interaction | No encouraged clicks, misleading ad labels or deceptive downloads |
| [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938?hl=en) | Inventory must provide publisher value | No ads on empty, construction, error or result-only screens |
| [Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) | No scaled low-value or misleading functionality | No fake utilities, doorway variants or copied portfolio pages |
| [Privacy disclosures](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437794?hl=en) | Explain actual ad-related collection/use | Publish provider-specific practices before activation |
| [CMP requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554020?hl=en-GB) | Regional consent requirements and certified CMP framework | Select/test appropriate current CMP and ad-serving configuration |

Search and AdSense are distinct systems. Search compliance does not establish AdSense approval.

## Inventory plan

Keep all tool routes free of third-party ad/analytics scripts, an internal privacy decision stricter than simply separating ads visually. Consider editorial articles and substantial hubs as candidate ad inventory after review. Home/directory ads must not mimic navigation or tools; initially leave them ad-free until usability is established. No ads on contact/error/legal flows.

This reduces revenue opportunity but avoids exposing user input to same-page third-party code. There is no need to force ads into the product merely because approval is a business objective.

## Submission readiness

- All advertised tools operate and export actual results; unsupported inputs get honest messages.
- Navigation and related destinations work; no public planned pages or thin archives.
- Original examples and guides explain the real implementation.
- About/contact identify the real operator and a usable support channel.
- Privacy/terms/cookie/disclaimer text reflects actual provider behavior.
- Hosting and ad integration are configured; if ads.txt is required by the chosen setup, use the actual publisher ID, never a fabricated one.
- Applicable consent behavior is verified for the selected regions and providers.
- Mobile layouts preserve content/actions; no overlay ads interfere with processing.
- Site owner reviews current account eligibility and policies before submission.

These are internal gates, not a promise Google will accept the site. Fifty-one content pages is the planned product scope, not a qualification threshold.

## Placement after approval

Ads remain visually identifiable and separated from editorial navigation. No ad-shaped tool actions, fake download controls, forced ad viewing, accidental-click traps or encouragement to click. Reserve layout space and test consent/ads on mobile and slow networks. Recheck privacy isolation before changing scripts. If ads make a page harder to use, remove or relocate them.

## Trust pages

/about/ explains purpose, operator and scope.
/contact/ provides a real channel.
/privacy-policy/ describes host logs, tool processing, reports, providers and retention.
/terms/ defines supported use, intellectual property and actual service limitations.
/cookie-policy/ reflects actual cookies/storage, including “none” only where verified.
/disclaimer/ explains approximation and compatibility limits without replacing useful tool-specific explanations.
/report-an-error/ lets users report a tool/version/problem without automatically transmitting their data.

Standalone /privacy/ and generic /methodology/ are not needed at launch. Methodology belongs on tool pages and the relevant original guides.
